Interactive dashboard visualizing the results of a full-factorial capacity-sweep simulation of the UK electricity grid's transition to Net Zero (2025–2050). Built as the companion visualization for my BEng final year project at King's College London.


What this shows

The underlying simulation evaluated 18,666 solar and wind capacity configurations across six milestone years, driven by a complete 8,784-hour meteorological dataset from eight UK Met Office MIDAS stations. This dashboard makes those results explorable rather than static, across five views:

Overview: Headline KPIs: renewable share, curtailment, solar/wind and conventional capacity, long-duration storage requirement, and adequacy deficit
Heatmaps: System performance across the full range of solar/wind capacity combinations for a selected year
Pathway: How key metrics evolve across the transition, year by year
Storage: Intra-day vs. long-duration storage requirement split
Adequacy: Reliability gap between annual decarbonisation progress and hourly grid adequacy

Key finding: Renewable generation reaches 84% of annual UK demand by 2050 — but annual sufficiency and hourly reliability are not the same property. Adequacy deficit grows from 7.7 TWh to 169.5 TWh over the same period, driven by a structural 40 GW gap between firm capacity and the firm demand floor. This dashboard is built to make that distinction visible and explorable, not just stated.


Related: Full methodology, results, and discussion are in the accompanying written report, "Pathways for 2050" (BEng Individual Project, King's College London, 2026).
